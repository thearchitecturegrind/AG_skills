# Brief Review
Reads a brief the way a skeptical project architect would and returns the gaps, contradictions, and hidden assumptions, plus questions to send the owner. A good result lets the team start with fewer surprises later.

## Ask first
1. Paste or attach the brief. [if it is only a verbal summary, treat everything as unconfirmed]
2. Who wrote it (owner, consultant, internal team) and what will it be used for (fee, design, procurement)? [assume design kickoff]
3. Is there a budget, schedule, or site document that should agree with it? [if missing, note that no cross-check was possible]

## Core rules
- Quote the brief when pointing at a problem; paraphrase invites argument about what it said.
- A conflict needs two quotes that cannot both be true; one vague phrase is a gap, not a conflict.
- Separate "not stated" from "stated vaguely" because they need different questions.
- List assumptions you would have to make to proceed; each is a risk someone must accept or confirm.
- Do not fill gaps with invented numbers; leave a marked placeholder or question.

## Workflow
1. Summarize the brief in five lines to check your understanding.
2. Walk the standard topics: purpose, users, area, budget, schedule, site, quality level, sustainability, approvals, who decides.
3. Record for each: Stated, Vague, or Missing, with the quote or page.
4. Find conflicts across topics (for example area versus budget).
5. List the assumptions needed to proceed and what happens if each is wrong.
6. Output: Summary, Gaps table, Conflicts, Assumptions, then a ready-to-send numbered question list for the owner, most consequential first.

## If your setup is different
- Very short brief: spend the effort on the questions list, not the table.
- Competition or RFP brief: add the evaluation criteria and what the submission must contain.
- Residential client: phrase questions in plain, non-technical language.

## Check the result
- [ ] Each gap or conflict cites a quote or says "absent".
- [ ] Questions are answerable by the owner, not by the architect.
- [ ] No invented requirements appear.
