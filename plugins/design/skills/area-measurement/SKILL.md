# Area Measurement
Determines which measurement standard a figure uses and what it includes or excludes, then reconciles differing numbers. A good result names the standard, its edition, and the rule that explains each difference.

## Ask first
1. What is the area figure for (lease, fee, code, cost estimate, planning limit)? [the purpose decides the standard]
2. Which standard or document governs it, and do you have its text? [if not, I will look it up from the official publisher and label what I cannot confirm]
3. Which areas disagree, and by how much? [ask for both numbers and their sources]

## Core rules
- Never quote inclusion rules (walls, shafts, balconies, parking) from memory; use the supplied text or the publisher's current edition, with edition and clause noted.
- Different purposes legitimately produce different areas; the goal is to explain the gap, not pick a winner.
- Check the measurement lines: inside face, outside face, centreline, or dominant face matters at every wall.
- Record the date and edition; standards are revised and old leases follow old editions.
- Area used for fees, code, or tax is for the responsible professional to confirm.

## Workflow
1. Name the purpose and the governing standard; cite the source and edition or write "unverified".
2. List what that standard includes and excludes, quoting the source.
3. Apply the rules to the floor plan: list each space type and whether it counts.
4. Recompute the area step by step, showing each addition and exclusion.
5. Compare with the other figures and attribute each difference to a rule.
6. Output: Standard and source, Inclusion table, Calculation, Reconciliation of differences, Open questions.

## If your setup is different
- Local or national standard instead of an international one: ask the user to name it and work from its text.
- Only a total is available: the reconciliation lists possible causes, marked as hypotheses.
- Metric versus imperial: convert once, show the factor, and say which unit is authoritative.

## Check the result
- [ ] Every inclusion rule has a source.
- [ ] The arithmetic is visible.
- [ ] Each difference is explained or marked unexplained.
