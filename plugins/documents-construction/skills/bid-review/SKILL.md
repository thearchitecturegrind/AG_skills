# Bid Review
Produces a bid comparison that levels scope and exposes exclusions, qualifications, and unusual terms. A good result shows which low bid is truly low and what to clarify before award.
## Ask first
1. What documents did bidders price (drawings, spec, addenda issued)? [ask for the list]
2. Delivery method and contract type (lump sum, GMP, cost-plus)? [lump sum]
3. Do you have the bid form and each bidder's qualifications letter? [ask; no letter means note it]
## Core rules
- Compare against the bid documents, not against each other; a gap shows only against the scope.
- Quote every exclusion and qualification; wording decides who pays.
- Do not recommend an award; present facts and risks for the owner and architect.
- Note addenda acknowledgment; a bid that ignores one priced old scope.
## Workflow
1. Build a scope checklist from the documents (divisions or trades).
2. Enter each bidder's price, alternates, unit prices, allowances, and duration.
3. Mark each scope item per bidder as included, excluded, or unclear, with page references.
4. List qualifications, clarifications, and terms that change risk (payment, escalation, liquidated damages edits).
5. Spot outliers: low or high lines and missing sections; list questions per bidder.
6. Output: leveling table, an exclusions list per bidder, and a clarification letter outline.
## If your setup is different
- If bids arrive in different formats, normalize to the bid form's line items and state assumptions.
- For public work, follow the owner's procurement rules; ask what applies.
- If only totals were given, say that a real comparison is not possible and what to request.
## Check the result
- [ ] Each exclusion quotes the bid with a page.
- [ ] Addenda acknowledgment is checked for every bidder.
- [ ] No award advice is given.
