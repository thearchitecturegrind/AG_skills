# View Analysis
Records what is visible from each important room, how good it is, and what could take it away. A good result lists views by room, rates them, and states which are secure and which depend on neighbours.

## Ask first
1. Which rooms matter and which window positions are fixed? [ask for the plan; if none, work from the list of rooms]
2. What is outside: neighbouring buildings, trees, landscape, landmarks, slopes? [ask for photos, survey, or a site section]
3. Does the owner own or control any of the land that holds the view? [assume not, and flag]

## Core rules
- Judge views from seated and standing eye heights, since a sill height can turn a view into a wall.
- Record what is seen at three depths: near, middle, far; a good view usually has depth.
- Separate what the site owner controls from what neighbours may build or grow; a view protected only by hope is not protected.
- Include views into the room (privacy) as well as out.
- Any legal view protection or right-to-light rule must come from a document you supply or an official source, cited.

## Workflow
1. List rooms and their windows with orientation and sill height.
2. For each, describe the view by depth and quality, with the source (photo, survey, plan).
3. Identify obstructions and what might change (planning, growth, development).
4. Rate each view as strong, fair, or poor, and secure or at risk.
5. Check overlooking between neighbours and from the public realm.
6. Output: room table (view, quality, risk, protecting factor), recommendations such as moving a window, framing, screening, or acquiring an easement, and verification tasks.

## If your setup is different
- No site photos: mark every view "unverified" and request photos from each window position.
- Urban setting: lead with overlooking and distance to neighbours.
- High-rise: ask for the surrounding massing and future development plans.

## Check the result
- [ ] Each view has a source.
- [ ] Risk to the view is stated, not omitted.
- [ ] Privacy was considered.
