# The Three Reads

Prompts for each pass through the brief. Not a checklist to fill in — use the ones that apply, skip the ones that don't.

---

## Read 1 — Intent

*What is this building for, and how will the owner know it worked?*

- Can you state the project's purpose in one sentence using only the brief's own words? If not, that's a finding.
- Are the priorities ranked? A list of equal priorities pushes the ranking onto the design team, where it shows up later as "that's not what we meant."
- What is the brief quiet about that this kind of client usually cares about? (A developer's exit value, a school district's bond schedule, a nonprofit's donor recognition, a corporation's recruiting story.)
- Are there words doing a lot of work without a definition — "flexible," "iconic," "welcoming," "sustainable," "future-proof"? Ask what each would look like if it succeeded.
- How will success be measured after occupancy, if at all?

## Read 2 — Quantities

*Every number the design will hang on.*

**Program**
- Areas per space, or just a list of rooms? Net or gross? Is a gross-up factor stated?
- Do the parts add up to the total?
- Headcount per space, and does it agree with the areas?
- Support space: circulation, MEP, IT, storage, loading, custodial, restrooms. Missing support space is the most common reason a building grows.
- Growth: expansion, shell space, phasing.
- Furniture and equipment: in scope, owner-furnished, or not mentioned?

**Place and rules**
- Address or parcel. Zoning district and overlays.
- Who the authority having jurisdiction is, by name.
- Code edition, including state and local amendments. "Applicable codes" is not an edition.
- Climate zone, utility providers and capacity, survey and geotech status.

**Performance**
- Energy, water, carbon, daylight, acoustic, air-quality targets — each with a metric and a number, or only an adjective?
- Certifications named with version? Do they carry prerequisites the brief excludes elsewhere (commissioning is the classic one)?
- "Net zero" — operational, embodied, or both? On-site, or offsets allowed?

**Money and time**
- Budget: construction only, or total project? Contingency? Escalation to the construction midpoint?
- Does the budget per square foot pass a sanity check against the user's benchmarks (`my-benchmarks.md`)? If no benchmark, say the check wasn't done.
- Schedule: does it include permit review, owner review periods, board cycles, and long-lead equipment?

## Read 3 — Responsibility

*Who decides, who pays, who provides, who carries the risk.*

- One named owner contact, or "the owner"? Who signs off at each phase, and is it a person or a committee?
- Board or public approvals: how often do they meet, how much lead time do they need?
- Explicit exclusions: what is out of scope, and does anything excluded contradict something required?
- Existing conditions: who verifies, and to what accuracy?
- Hazardous materials: is there an environmental assessment? For older buildings, assume something will be found.
- Geotech and survey: who commissions and pays, and when?
- Permit and impact fees: whose budget?
- Owner-furnished items (AV, IT, signage, art, FF&E): who coordinates them into the drawings?
- Climate hazards the site actually faces (flood, heat, wind, fire, seismic): are they named, or is "resilient" just an adjective?

---

## How brief type shifts emphasis

| Type | Lean hardest on |
|---|---|
| RFP | Responsibility (scope, exclusions, decision-makers) and whether the fee basis matches the scope |
| OPR | Performance — every target measurable and verifiable at commissioning |
| Program | Quantities — areas, headcounts, adjacencies, support space |
| Design brief | Intent — undefined adjectives, unranked priorities |
