# Existing Conditions Survey
Turns scattered observations into an organized record that separates what was measured, what was seen, and what is assumed. A good result tells the design team exactly what they can rely on and what needs another visit.

## Ask first
1. What material do you have (field notes, photos, old drawings, measured sketches)? [if drawings only, everything is "from record, unverified"]
2. What is the purpose (renovation, addition, tenant fit-out, condition assessment)? [the purpose decides how much accuracy matters]
3. Which areas were accessed, and which were hidden (ceilings, roof, behind finishes)? [ask for a list]

## Core rules
- Label every item Measured, Observed, From drawings, or Assumed; mixing these is how design errors start.
- Cite the source (photo number, sheet, note date) for every record.
- Report what is not accessible and what it might hide, such as services, structure, or hazardous materials.
- Do not diagnose structure, moisture, or hazardous materials; list signs and refer to the specialist.
- Record dimensions with their measurement basis and tolerance when known.

## Workflow
1. Group the material by area or system: structure, envelope, interior, services, site.
2. For each item write the condition, its status label, and its source.
3. Compare record drawings with observations and list discrepancies.
4. List what is unverified and the method that would verify it (opening-up, scan, test, specialist).
5. Prioritize which unknowns could change the design or cost most.
6. Output: record table, discrepancy list, unverified list with proposed method, and a recommended second visit plan.

## If your setup is different
- Photos only: describe visible conditions and request dimensions.
- Scan or point-cloud data: state the accuracy provided and the areas not covered.
- Hazardous-material suspicion: stop at flagging and name the specialist survey required.

## Check the result
- [ ] Each entry has a status label and a source.
- [ ] Unverified areas are listed with a way to check them.
- [ ] No diagnosis is given.
