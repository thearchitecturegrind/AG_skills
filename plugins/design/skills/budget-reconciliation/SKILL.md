# Budget Reconciliation
Compares what the programme asks for with what the budget can buy, then offers ways to close the gap with the consequences of each. A good result shows the arithmetic, the cost source, and the choices the client must make.

## Ask first
1. What are the programme areas and the budget, and is the budget construction only or total project cost? [if unclear, ask; the difference can be 20 percent or more]
2. What unit costs can I use, and where do they come from (estimator, past tenders, published cost data)? [if none, I leave cost cells as placeholders and will not guess]
3. What is fixed and what can flex (area, quality, scope, phasing)? [assume nothing is fixed]

## Core rules
- Use only supplied or sourced unit rates, with date, location basis, and what they include; cost figures from memory are unreliable and date quickly.
- Carry contingency, fees, and escalation as separate visible lines, so the gap is not hidden inside rates.
- Price by element (shell, interiors, services, sitework) so cuts can be targeted.
- Every proposed saving states what the client gives up; a cut with no consequence is not a real option.
- Estimates here are for early decisions; a cost consultant should confirm.

## Workflow
1. Restate budget basis and programme areas; show the area total.
2. Build the cost table: element, quantity, rate, source, subtotal.
3. Add contingency, fees, and escalation as stated by the user.
4. Compute the gap in money and in percent of budget.
5. List closing options (reduce area, simplify form, change specification, phase), each with estimated saving from the table and the consequence.
6. Output: Basis, Cost table, Gap, Options table, Recommended order of decisions, Assumptions to confirm.

## If your setup is different
- No rates available: deliver the structure and the quantities, with blank rate cells and a request for the estimator's figures.
- Fixed-price design-build: add risk and scope-change lines.
- Different currency: keep one currency and state any conversion and its date.

## Check the result
- [ ] Every rate has a source and date, or is blank.
- [ ] The gap arithmetic can be followed line by line.
- [ ] Each saving lists its consequence.
