# Delay Triage
Produces a fact timeline and a clause map for a delay, with open questions. A good result helps the team prepare notices and records; it does not decide liability.
## Ask first
1. What is the delay (activity, dates, length)? [ask]
2. Do you have the contract, schedule, daily reports, and notices? [ask which exist]
3. Who is asking (owner, architect, contractor)? [ask]
## Core rules
- Gather facts before conclusions; liability depends on contract wording and evidence.
- Quote clauses the user supplies; do not state notice periods from memory.
- Check critical path; a delay to non-critical work may not extend completion.
- This is fact-gathering, not legal advice; recommend counsel for claims.
## Workflow
1. Build a dated timeline from reports, emails, RFIs, and submittals with source for each event.
2. Identify the affected activities and whether the schedule shows them on the critical path.
3. List possible causes (design information, owner decision, weather, contractor resource, supply).
4. Map each to contract clauses on delay, notice, extension, and cost, as supplied.
5. Note notice dates against the clause requirements.
6. Output: timeline table, clause map, evidence gaps, and questions for counsel.
## If your setup is different
- If there is no contract text, list clause types to look for and mark everything unverified.
- If the schedule is missing, say causation cannot be assessed.
- If jurisdiction affects notices, ask the user to confirm the law with counsel.
## Check the result
- [ ] Each fact cites a document.
- [ ] Clauses are quoted, not recalled.
- [ ] No finding of fault is stated.
