# Security Design Review
Reads a plan as a user would, including a vulnerable one, and lists where the design supports or undermines safety and control. A good result stays at design level, with no instructions for defeating protections.

## Ask first
1. What is the building and who uses it, when, and who is most vulnerable (children, night staff, patients)? [ask; risk depends on users]
2. What risks does the owner worry about (theft, harassment, vandalism, unauthorized entry)? [if unknown, ask for the owner's concerns and the incident history]
3. What is already planned: management, staffing, lighting, cameras, access control? [assume none and say so]

## Core rules
- Frame as design for safety: sightlines, clear ownership of space, controlled access points, and good lighting; these also make a place pleasant.
- Review the experience at the worst times (late, empty, closing time), not only at peak hours.
- Keep outputs at the level of design concerns and improvements; do not describe how to bypass locks, alarms, or surveillance.
- Balance security with escape and accessibility; locked or hidden routes can endanger people in an emergency.
- The security consultant and licensed professional determine the final measures; this review is advisory.

## Workflow
1. Map entries, boundaries, and who can reach what.
2. Walk the plan as a visitor, staff member, and a person alone at night.
3. List concern points: hidden recesses, long blind corridors, unsupervised entrances, ambiguous public or private edges, concealed deliveries.
4. Judge each as low, medium, or high concern with the reason and a plan location.
5. Propose design improvements: visibility, layout changes, clear thresholds, lighting zones, management measures.
6. Output: Concern table (location, issue, level, improvement), Strengths, Questions for the owner.

## If your setup is different
- School or healthcare: ask for the sector's own guidance and quote it.
- Outdoor space: add approach routes, lighting continuity, and escape paths.
- Request for ways to get past the protection: decline that part and stay with design improvements.

## Check the result
- [ ] Each concern has a plan location.
- [ ] Escape and accessibility were considered.
- [ ] No bypass methods appear.
