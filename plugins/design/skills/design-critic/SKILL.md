# Design Critic
Produces a structured critique that separates what the design sets out to do from how well it does it. A good result names a few strong moves, a few real problems, and the question to settle next.

## Ask first
1. What is the design trying to achieve, in the author's words? [if unstated, infer it and mark "inferred"]
2. Who is the critique for, and at what stage (concept, schematic, final)? [early stage: judge the idea, not the finishes]
3. Which materials do you have: plans, sections, renders, a written brief? [if only an image, say the critique covers what is visible and nothing else]

## Core rules
- Critique against the stated intent first; a design should be judged on its own terms before outside taste enters.
- Tie every comment to something visible (sheet, view, room, or line of text); vague praise or blame cannot be acted on.
- Name what cannot be judged from the material supplied, so silence is not read as approval.
- Rank comments by consequence; ten equal bullets hide the one issue that matters.
- Treat anything about structure, fire, or accessibility as a flag for the licensed professional, not a verdict.

## Workflow
1. Restate the intent and the three decisions the design leans on most.
2. List strengths: where the design delivers its intent, with the evidence.
3. List problems: where it contradicts its intent, its brief, or itself.
4. List unknowns: questions the material cannot answer.
5. Rank the problems and propose one concrete next move for each of the top three.
6. Output: Intent, Strengths, Problems (ranked, each with source), Unknowns, Next moves. Keep it to one page.

## If your setup is different
- No stated intent: ask once; if still none, critique for internal consistency only and say so.
- Student or studio setting: add a short "questions to bring to the crit" list instead of directives.
- Client-facing review: soften nothing in the findings, but separate "fix" items from "discuss" items.

## Check the result
- [ ] Every problem points to a view, room, or line of the supplied material.
- [ ] At least one strength is real and specific, not filler.
- [ ] The unknowns section exists and is honest.
