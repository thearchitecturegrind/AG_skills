# Design Interior
Produces a short interior definition: who uses the space, what it must do, how it should feel, and what limits it. A good result gives later decisions on finishes and furniture something to be judged against.

## Ask first
1. What is the space, who uses it, and for what activities and hours? [ask; do not assume]
2. What feeling or identity should it carry, and what references exist? [if none, propose three words and ask for confirmation]
3. What are the fixed limits: budget, existing shell, services, maintenance, schedule? [mark unknowns]

## Core rules
- Describe functions before finishes; a pretty room that does not work gets redecorated.
- Turn feelings into observable design moves (light level, acoustics, texture, colour range) so they can be checked.
- List performance needs: acoustics, cleaning, durability, lighting, comfort; get targets from the user or a cited standard.
- Record what is fixed (windows, structure, services) apart from what can change.
- Fire, accessibility, and material safety requirements come from the project team and the authority; flag them.

## Workflow
1. Write a one-paragraph statement of purpose.
2. List activities and the settings each needs (seating, storage, privacy, services).
3. Translate the desired atmosphere into design moves with a test for each.
4. List performance needs and constraints with sources.
5. Identify decisions to make next and in which order (layout, lighting, materials, furniture).
6. Output: Statement, Activities table, Atmosphere moves, Performance and constraints, Decision order, Open questions.

## If your setup is different
- Residential client: write in the client's own words and keep terms plain.
- Commercial or hospitality brand: ask for the brand guidelines and quote them.
- Early-stage with no site data: deliver the statement and questions only.

## Check the result
- [ ] Each feeling is linked to a measurable move.
- [ ] Constraints are cited or marked unknown.
- [ ] No finishes were chosen yet.
