# Photo Reader
Produces a careful description of a photo with visible facts, limits of the image, and follow-up questions. A good result never claims more than the picture proves.
## Ask first
1. What is the photo supposed to document and where was it taken? [ask]
2. Do you have the drawing or spec it relates to? [offer to compare]
3. When was it taken and by whom? [unknown; note]
## Core rules
- Describe only what is visible; hidden conditions behind finishes cannot be judged.
- State limits plainly: no scale, angle, lighting, or cropped areas.
- Never declare something code-compliant or structurally sound; refer to the licensed professional.
- Ask for another angle when it would settle the question.
## Workflow
1. Identify the likely area and stage of work (framing, rough-in, finishes) with a confidence level.
2. List visible elements: materials, equipment, conditions, damage, workmanship.
3. List what cannot be seen or measured from the image.
4. If drawings are supplied, compare and note differences with sheet references.
5. Suggest additional photos or measurements to confirm.
6. Output: Visible, Not visible, Possible concerns (for professional review), Suggested follow-up.
## If your setup is different
- If the photo is blurry or dark, say what could not be read.
- If several photos are given, number them and describe each separately.
- For safety hazards, advise informing the responsible person right away.
## Check the result
- [ ] Visible and not-visible items are separated.
- [ ] No compliance or structural conclusions are stated.
- [ ] Follow-up photos or questions are listed.
