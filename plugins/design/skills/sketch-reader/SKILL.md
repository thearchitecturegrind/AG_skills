# Sketch Reader
Interprets a loose drawing, rendering, or model view and states what it fixes, what it suggests, and what is still open. A good result separates evidence in the image from the viewer's imagination.

## Ask first
1. What is the image supposed to show, and who made it? [if unknown, state what you infer]
2. What stage is it (first idea, option, near-final)? [assume early]
3. Is there any scale, text, or context outside the image? [if none, no dimensions can be claimed]

## Core rules
- Report what is drawn before what is meant; a sketch line is evidence, a guess about intent is interpretation, and they must stay labelled.
- Rendered images are persuasive and often more committed than they look; list what they imply without proving.
- Do not measure from an image without a known reference; perspective distorts.
- Note what the view conceals (angle, crop, atmosphere) because the choice of view is itself a decision.
- Do not turn a sketch into a statement about structure or compliance.

## Workflow
1. Describe the image literally: elements, relationships, evident scale cues.
2. List commitments: massing, openings, circulation, material hints, that the image clearly fixes.
3. List suggestions: things implied but not fixed.
4. List open questions the image cannot answer, and what drawing would answer each.
5. Name what the choice of viewpoint hides.
6. Output: Literal description, Commitments, Suggestions, Open questions, Next drawing to make.

## If your setup is different
- Hand sketch with annotations: treat the notes as separate evidence and quote them.
- 3D model view: ask for the other views, since one angle can mislead.
- AI-generated or stylized render: warn that details may not be buildable and treat them as mood, not design.

## Check the result
- [ ] Description and interpretation are kept apart.
- [ ] No measurement was taken without a scale cue.
- [ ] Open questions each name a drawing that would settle them.
